Channel Charting-Based Channel Prediction on
Real-World Distributed Massive MIMO CSI
Phillip Stephan , Florian Euchner , Stephan ten Brink
Institute of Telecommunications, Pfaffenwaldring 47, University of Stuttgart, 70569 Stuttgart, Germany

Abstract—Distributed massive MIMO is considered a key
advancement for improving the performance of next-generation
wireless telecommunication systems. However, its efficacy in
scenarios involving user mobility is limited due to channel
aging. To address this challenge, channel prediction techniques
are investigated to forecast future channel state information
(CSI) based on previous estimates. We propose a new channel
prediction method based on channel charting, a self-supervised
learning technique that reconstructs a physically meaningful
latent representation of the radio environment using similarity
relationships between CSI samples. The concept of inertia within
a channel chart allows for predictive radio resource management
tasks through the latent space. We demonstrate that channel
charting can be used to predict future CSI by exploiting spatial
relationships between known estimates that are embedded in
the channel chart. Our method is validated on a real-world
distributed massive MIMO dataset, and compared to a Wiener
predictor and the outdated CSI in terms of achievable sum rate.
Index Terms —AI, channel aging, channel charting, channel
prediction, cell-free, distributed, massive MIMO
I. I NTRODUCTION
Sixth generation (6G) wireless communication systems need
to deploy new technologies to achieve the ambitious require-
ments in terms of data throughput, latency and energy con-
sumption. Distributed massive multiple-input multiple-output
(MIMO), also known as cell-free massive MIMO, is seen as
a key enabler for spatial multiplexing by installing numerous
spatially distributed antennas at the base station (BS). To fully
exploit their potential, these systems rely on the availability
of timely channel state information (CSI). Operating in time
division duplex (TDD) mode, the estimation of uplink (UL)
CSI at the BS is sufficient for downlink (DL) communication
because of the reciprocity between UL and DL channel.
However, rapid changes of the wireless channel due to user
equipment (UE) mobility or moving objects lead to outdated
CSI estimates at the BS. This phenomenon is well studied and
commonly referred to as channel aging [1]–[3].
To mitigate the decline in DL communication performance
caused by channel aging, while avoiding additional com-
munication overhead from increasing the frequency of CSI
estimation, strategies for channel prediction are investigated.

V. C HANNEL CHARTING -BASED CHANNEL PREDICTION
The three major steps of our channel prediction method
are visualized in Fig. 3. At first, the forward charting function
(FCF) is learned on Dtrain and the estimated channel chart po-
sitions are stored at the BS. Then, the channel chart positions
on Dpred are inferred from the FCF. Inertia within the channel
chart allows for predicting future positions from a sequence
of previous ones. In the third step, Delauny triangulation is
applied to the channel chart positions of Dtrain to create a
mesh of triangles. If the predicted position from the previous
step lies within a triangle, the CSI is predicted based on linear
interpolation between the triangle’s vertices. Fig. 1 illustrates
the concept of this method comprehensibly. Further details on
the individual steps are provided in the following sections.
A. Dissimilarity Metric-Based Channel Charting
Our approach for dissimilarity metric-based channel chart-
ing, as described in [20], relies on dissimilarities di,j (”pseudo-
distances”) between any pair of datapoints with indices i and
j in the training set, to learn a channel chart jointly for all
arrays. In particular, the geodesic, fused dissimilarity metric
[20] is employed, which computes dissimilarities based on
the angle-delay profile (ADP) at all BS antenna arrays and
information about timestamp differences. Learning the FCF,
which maps the high-dimensional CSI to the low-dimensional
latent space, i.e., the channel chart, is an optimization problem
that minimizes the error between the dissimilarity matrix and
the point-to-point distances in the channel chart. Since no
ground truth information is available, the learned channel chart
embodies the radio environment in a transformed version of
the physical coordinates. We implement the FCF as a deep
neural network (DNN) CΘ : f(l) → z(l), where f(l) is a CSI
feature derived from H(l) serving as DNN input, and z(l) is
the respective estimated channel chart position. As described
in [20], we use sample autocorrelations of time-domain CSI
